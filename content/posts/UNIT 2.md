## UNIT 2

Unit 2 pushed me to think about information as not just something we consume and have to organise but as something we need to **model, transform, and optimise**. The classes we had covered a lot of ground (which was overwhelming at times) image transformations, optimisation problems, and the ideas of **modelling and abstraction** and while some of it was definitely challenging, it was also where things started to come together and fell more concrete. 

The lab on **image transformations** was a good example of this. Working on pixel values with piece wise functions and bitwise operations like **XOR** which we also briefly covered in the first semester made me realise how much mathematics sits underneath something as every day as a photo. I was glad to begin learning this topic because we finally moved on from abstract theoretical learning to actually making sense of things in reality.

The **optimization sessions** looked at problems like the travelling salesman and the bridge-crossing puzzle which showed me that finding the **"best"** solution is rarely straightforward, and that choosing between approaches like **greedy algorithms and dynamic programming** actually matters since the wrong option can cost you significantly. The group lab on logistics network optimisation proved this practically. I found this topic fascinating and went down a rabbit hole watching YouTube videos on other similar problems for hours!

What tied everything together was the reading on **abstraction** which allows us to strip a problem down and find exactly what is needed while leaving the unnecessary details, which is why I think after this unit it is the most important skill in computing or writing a program. It allows us to find differences and disgusting between an okay and a great solution to a problem
