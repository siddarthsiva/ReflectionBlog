UNIT 2

Unit 2 pushed me to think about information as not just something we consume and have to organise but as something we need to model, transform and optimise. The classes we had covered a lot of ground (which was overwhelming at times) image transformations, optimisation problems, and the ideas of modelling and abstraction and while some of it was definitely challenging, it was also where things started to come together and fell more concrete.

The lab on image transformations was a good example of this. Working on pixel values with piece wise functions and bit wise operations like XOR which we also briefly covered in the first semester made me realise how much mathematics sits underneath something as every day as a photo. It gave me a moment of realisation where theory stopped feeling abstract and started to make sense in real context.

The optimization sessions were equally interesting. Looking at problems like the travelling salesman and the bridge-crossing puzzle showed me that finding the “best” solution is rarely straightforward, and that choosing between approaches like greedy algorithms and dynamic programming actually matters since the wrong choice can cost you significantly and the group lab on logistics network optimisation drove this home in a very practical way. I found this topic fascinating and went down a rabbit hole watching YouTube videos son other similar projects for hours!

What tied everything together was the reading on abstraction . The argument that abstraction is the key skill in computing stuck with me, the idea that being able to strip a problem down to what actually matters, rather than getting lost in the details, is what separates a good solution from a convoluted one. That feels like a skill worth actively trying to build, not just in information sciences but beyond it as well.
